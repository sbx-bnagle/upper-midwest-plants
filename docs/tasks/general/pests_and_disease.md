#### Watching for pests and disease

Almost everything that goes wrong in a garden is easier to deal with early, and
early means before it is visible from the path.

Walk the garden once a week in the growing season and turn a few leaves over as
you go. Aphids, mites, and the first spots of mildew all show on the undersides
first. Look at new growth, which is what most pests go for, and at anything that
looks different from its neighbors.

Most problems are solved without spraying. Aphids come off with a jet of water.
Diseased leaves are removed and binned. Mildew is usually a symptom of poor
airflow or overhead watering, and both of those are yours to change. Slugs are
trapped or handpicked in the evening.

A plant that gets the same trouble every year in the same spot is telling you it
is in the wrong place. Moving it is a better answer than treating it annually.

Some damage is not worth acting on. A few chewed leaves in a garden with
predators in it is the system working.

##### Tools
- Hand lens, useful but not required
- Snips and a bag, for removing affected growth
- Hose with a jet nozzle
