#### Mulching

Mulch is a layer of shredded bark, leaf litter, or compost laid over open soil.
It holds moisture, keeps weed seeds dark, moderates soil temperature, and feeds
the soil as it breaks down.

Lay it 2–3in deep across the bed, and keep it back about 2in from stems and
trunks. Refresh as it thins, usually every year or two. Shredded bark lasts
longest. Leaf litter and compost break down faster and do more for the soil.

Spring mulching is for moisture and weeds. Late fall mulching, put down after
the ground has frozen, is for temperature: it holds the soil cold and steady so
a February thaw does not lift crowns out of the ground.

Mulch piled against a stem keeps the bark permanently wet, which rots it, and
gives voles somewhere to spend the winter chewing. Laid thicker than about 4in
it sheds rain rather than letting it through.

There is no precision needed here. Two or three inches is about the depth of a
spread hand, and that is close enough.
