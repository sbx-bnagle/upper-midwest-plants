#### Fall cleanup

The instinct in fall is to cut everything down and rake the beds bare. Most of
that work is better left undone.

Standing stems hold overwintering bees and pupae, seedheads feed birds through
winter, and leaf litter shelters both. A bed cleared to bare soil in October has
none of that, and gains nothing except a tidy look for the four months when
nobody is in the garden.

What is worth doing: remove anything diseased, since spores overwinter in the
debris and start next year’s infection. Cut back plants that flop into paths.
Pull weeds before they seed. Empty and store containers before they freeze and
crack, drain and coil hoses, and mulch new plantings once the ground has frozen
to stop them heaving.

Leave the rest until spring. The fallen leaves can stay on the beds; they are
the mulch, and they break down into the soil by summer.

This is the rare case where doing less is the better practice rather than the
lazier one.

##### Tools
- Hand pruners
- Rake, for paths and lawn rather than beds
- Bags for diseased material
