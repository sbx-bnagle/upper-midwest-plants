#### Feeding

Most established perennial beds do not need feeding. A plant that is not growing
well is more often short of water, light, or root space than of nutrients, and
fertilizer will not fix any of those.

Where it is warranted, an inch of compost spread over the bed in spring is
enough for the year. It releases slowly, improves the soil as it breaks down,
and cannot burn roots. Granular fertilizer suits containers and heavy feeders,
applied in spring at the rate on the box.

Do not feed after midsummer. Late feeding pushes soft growth that has not
hardened when the first frost arrives, and that growth dies back and takes some
of the plant with it.

Overfeeding is the more common error, and it shows as tall, soft, floppy growth
with few flowers. Native perennials in particular flower better in lean soil
than in rich, and feeding them works against the thing you planted them for.

If you are unsure, do nothing for a season and watch. That is a real diagnostic
rather than a way of avoiding the decision.
