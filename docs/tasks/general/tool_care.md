#### Tool care

A sharp, clean tool makes a cut that heals. A blunt one crushes the stem, and a
dirty one moves disease from plant to plant.

Wipe the blades down after each session and dry them before they go away. Sharpen
pruners and shears a few times through the season with a file or a sharpening
stone, working the beveled side only. Oil the pivot and the springs. At the end
of the year, clean everything properly, oil the metal, and rub linseed oil into
wooden handles.

Disinfect between plants when you are cutting out disease, and between shrubs
when pruning. Wiping the blade with rubbing alcohol takes seconds and stops you
carrying a canker down a whole hedge.

Blunt pruners tear rather than slice, and a torn cut takes longer to seal and
lets in what is waiting. Most people notice this as the tool getting harder to
squeeze before they notice it as damage to the plant.

Sharpening is a five minute job and does not require skill. Follow the angle
that is already there.

##### Tools
- File or sharpening stone
- Rubbing alcohol or disinfectant wipes
- Machine oil
- Linseed oil, for wooden handles
- Wire brush, for rust
