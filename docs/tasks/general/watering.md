#### Watering

Most gardens are watered too often and too lightly. The habit to build is the
opposite: less often, and enough to soak through when you do.

Check before you water. Push a finger into the soil, or lever it open with a
spade. If it is damp 2in down, wait. When you do water, aim to wet the top 6–9in
and then leave it until it dries again. Water in the early morning, at the base
of the plant rather than over the leaves, and slowly enough that it soaks in
rather than running off.

New plantings are the exception and want steady moisture through their first
season, before they move onto this schedule.

Frequent shallow watering keeps roots in the top inch of soil, where they cook
in a hot July and dry out within days of the last rain. Deep watering sends
roots down, and a deeply rooted garden gets through a dry fortnight without you.

Sandy soil drains faster and wants smaller amounts more often. Clay holds water
and wants less, but takes it slowly, so run the hose gently or it will sheet off
the surface.

##### Tools
- Soaker hose or drip line
- Watering can for spot work
- Spade, for checking depth
