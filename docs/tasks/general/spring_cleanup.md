#### Spring cleanup

Spring cleanup is cutting down last year’s growth and clearing the beds for the
season.

Wait longer than feels natural. The stems and leaf litter you are about to
remove are still holding overwintering insects, and they leave as temperatures
settle. Mid to late April is usually right, once there is green at the crowns
and the ground has dried enough to walk on without compacting it.

Cut perennials down to 8–24in of stubble rather than flush to the ground, and
leave the cut stems somewhere out of the way for a few weeks so anything still
inside can emerge. Pull the winter’s weeds while the ground is soft. Refresh
mulch once you can see what you are working around.

Going in too early costs you a generation of native bees and does the plants no
good either, since a hard freeze after cutting exposes crowns that the old growth
was protecting. Going in too late means working around new shoots, which is slow
and easy to damage.

Working on soggy ground compacts it for the rest of the year. If your boot
leaves a print, come back in a few days.

##### Tools
- Hand pruners and hedge shears
- Garden fork
- Rake
- Tarp or barrow
