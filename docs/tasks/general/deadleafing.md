#### Deadleafing

Over a season, every plant sheds some leaves to damage, heat, or age.
Deadleafing is taking those off as you notice them.

Remove the whole leaf, stalk and all, back to the crown or the stem it grows
from. A leaf torn off halfway leaves a stub that browns in turn and has to be
done again. This is not scheduled work; do it while you are walking the garden
for something else.

Leaves marked with spots or powdery patches are carrying the disease that made
them, so those go in the bin rather than the compost heap. Left lying under the
plant, they reinfect the new growth above them every time it rains.

Plants tolerate this far better than it looks. Removing a third of the foliage
from a healthy plant does not check it.

The change in how a bed reads is out of proportion to the effort. Most of what
makes a planting look tired in August is dead foliage rather than anything
wrong with the plants.

##### Tools
- Snips or hand pruners
- Gloves
- A bag for diseased material
