#### Cut back

Cutting back is removing last year’s stems down to a short stub at the base.

Do it in early spring, once there is green at the crown. The stems died months
ago, so the plant loses nothing. Leave 8–24in of stubble rather than cutting
flush. Hollow stems at that height are where stem‑nesting bees build, and new
foliage covers the stubs within a few weeks.

Cutting in fall costs you that habitat and returns nothing. Leaving it undone
costs less: the clump grows up through the old stems and looks untidy by
midsummer.

The cut does not need to be neat. Nothing you remove is alive.

##### Tools
- Hand pruners
- Hedge shears, for clumps wider than about 2ft
- Gloves
