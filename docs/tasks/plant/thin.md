#### Thin

Thinning is taking whole stems out at ground level to open up a crowded plant,
rather than shortening the stems that stay.

Take the oldest and thickest stems first, cutting as close to the base as the
tool will reach. Remove up to a third in any one year. On a shrub that has gone
woody and bare at the bottom, repeating this over three years replaces the whole
plant with new growth from the roots without ever leaving a gap.

Take more than a third and the plant answers with a thicket of weak shoots.
Thin nothing and the center stays dark and still, which is where mildew and
scale start.

Choosing which stems to remove is easier than it sounds. Oldest, thickest,
or growing back through the middle.

##### Tools
- Loppers
- A pruning saw for old stems at the base
- Gloves
