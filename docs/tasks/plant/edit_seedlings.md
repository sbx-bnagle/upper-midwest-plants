#### Thin seedlings

Some plants seed themselves freely. Thinning seedlings is pulling out most of
the volunteers and keeping a few.

Do it in late spring, when the seedlings are big enough to recognize but small
enough to come up with a light pull. Damp ground releases them cleanly. Keep the
ones standing where you would have planted them and pull the rest, including
every one that has come up inside another plant’s crown.

Left alone, a strong self‑seeder crowds out its neighbors within two or three
seasons, and the plants it displaces are usually the ones you paid most for.
Pull every last seedling and you lose the free replacements, which matters for
short‑lived plants that will not be there in three years.

Getting the number wrong in either direction is easy to correct next spring.

##### Tools
- Gloves
- A hand fork for seedlings that resist
