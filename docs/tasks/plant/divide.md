#### Divide

Dividing is lifting a clump out of the ground, splitting it into pieces, and
replanting them.

A plant asks for it before you decide to do it. The middle of the clump dies out
and the growth rings the edge, the flowers come smaller than they did, or the
whole thing blooms less. Any of those means the clump has outgrown its own root
mass.

Divide in early spring, as growth is starting and the shoots are still short
enough to work around. Water the ground the day before if it is dry. Lift the
whole clump with a fork, shake the loose soil off the roots, and split it. Small
crowns pull apart in your hands. Dense ones want a spade driven through, or two
forks back to back and levered apart, and neither of these will hurt the plant
as much as it appears to. Keep pieces with three to five healthy shoots and a
decent share of root, and compost the worn‑out middle. Replant at the same depth
the clump was growing, water in, and keep the ground damp for a few weeks.

Divide in fall instead and the pieces have four to six weeks to root before
the ground freezes. Miss that window and they heave out over winter. Divide
nothing and the clump keeps going, flowering less each year, until the center is
bare.

Spring‑divided pieces have the whole season to recover, which is why spring is
the safer choice if you are doing this for the first time.

##### Tools
- Garden fork, or two for levering
- Spade
- Hand pruners for tough crowns
- Watering can or hose
