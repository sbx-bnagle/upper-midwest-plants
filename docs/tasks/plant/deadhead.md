#### Deadhead

Deadheading is cutting off a flower once it has finished, before it sets seed.

Cut the stem back to the first full leaf or side bud below the spent bloom, not
just the petals. Work through the plant every week or two while it is in flower.
On plants that rebloom, this brings a second flush. On plants that do not, it
stops the plant spending itself on seed.

Leave the last flush of the season alone. Those seed heads feed birds through
winter and are worth more standing than cut.

Skip deadheading entirely and a heavy self‑seeder will put volunteers through
the bed by next spring. That is a slower problem than it sounds, and pulling
seedlings is easy.

##### Tools
- Hand pruners, or snips for thin stems
- A bucket or tarp for what you cut
