#### Prune

Pruning is making structural cuts on woody stems: shortening them, or taking
them out where they join another branch.

Before you cut, settle one question. Does this shrub flower on old wood, meaning
the stems that grew last year, or on new wood, meaning the stems it will grow
this season? A shrub that flowers on old wood is carrying next spring’s buds
right now, and pruning it in late winter removes them. Prune those within two
weeks of flowering finishing, and no later. A shrub that flowers on new wood, or
one grown for its leaves rather than flowers, is pruned in late winter or early
spring before growth starts, while the branch structure is still visible.

Cut back to just above a bud facing away from the center of the plant, or take
the stem right out at its base. Do not leave stubs between the two. A stub has
no bud to grow from and dies back, and the dieback carries on into live wood.

Start with anything dead, damaged, or crossing another branch. That much is
always safe, needs no decision about flowering wood, and on many shrubs it is
the only pruning wanted in a given year.

Prune at the wrong time and you lose a season of flowers. The shrub itself is
rarely harmed, and it will flower again the following year.

##### Tools
- Hand pruners, for stems up to about ¾in
- Loppers, for stems up to about 1½in
- A pruning saw for anything thicker
- Gloves
