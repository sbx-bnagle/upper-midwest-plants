#### Watch for problems

Some plants reliably get a particular pest or disease. Watching means looking for
it early, when it is still small enough to deal with.

Walk the planting once a week through the growing season and turn a few leaves
over. Most trouble shows on the underside first, and most of it is easier to stop
at the point where you have to look for it than at the point where it is obvious
from the path.

What you are looking for depends on the plant, and the plant’s own guidance will
name it: mildew on phlox and bee balm in humid weather, slugs on hosta as the
leaves unfurl, leaf spot after a wet spring.

Caught early, most of these are handled by removing the affected leaves, opening
up the planting for airflow, or changing how you water. Caught late, the options
narrow to spraying or replacing the plant.

A weekly walk is not an inspection. It is the same walk you were going to take
anyway, with your eyes down.
