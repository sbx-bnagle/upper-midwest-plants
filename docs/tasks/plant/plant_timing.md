#### When to plant

Most perennials go in either in spring or in early fall, and which one suits a
given plant depends on when it does its root growing.

Spring planting suits anything that flowers late, and anything with fleshy roots
that rot in cold wet ground. The plant has the whole season to settle. Fall
planting suits spring bloomers and most shrubs, and it works because the soil is
still warm while the air has cooled, which is the condition roots grow in.

Plant in fall four to six weeks before the ground freezes. Any later and the
roots have not gripped, and the freeze‑thaw cycles of the following spring lift
the plant clean out of the ground.

Midsummer is the window to avoid. A plant set out in July spends its first weeks
losing water through its leaves faster than a small root ball can replace it, and
it will need watering every few days to survive at all.

Neither spring nor fall is a narrow window. Several weeks either side of ideal
still works.
