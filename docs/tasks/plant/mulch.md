#### Mulch

Mulch is a layer of shredded bark, leaf litter, or compost spread over the soil
around a plant.

Lay it 2–3in deep, out to the edge of the plant’s spread, and keep it back about
2in from the stems or trunk. Refresh it as it breaks down, usually every year or
two.

Timing depends on what you want from it. Mulch laid in spring holds moisture and
keeps weed seeds dark through the growing season. Mulch laid in late fall, after
the ground has frozen, is doing something different: it holds the soil
temperature steady so that a February thaw does not lift the crown out of the
ground and leave the roots in the air.

Piled against the stems it keeps the bark wet, which rots it and invites rodents
to spend the winter chewing on it. Laid too thick, over about 4in, it sheds
rain instead of holding it.

Depth by eye is close enough. Two or three inches is roughly the depth of a
spread hand.

##### Tools
- Wheelbarrow or tub
- Garden fork or shovel
- Gloves
