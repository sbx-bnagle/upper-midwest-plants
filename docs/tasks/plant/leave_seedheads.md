#### Leave seedheads

Leaving seedheads means not deadheading the last flush of flowers, so the plant
sets and keeps its seed.

Goldfinches, juncos, and chickadees feed on standing seed through the winter,
and coneflower, black‑eyed susan, and the grasses hold theirs well into
February. Deadhead in September and that food is gone before it is needed.

Stop deadheading in late summer, once the plant’s main display is over. From
there, leave everything standing until spring.

The trade is self‑seeding. A plant left to hold its seed will drop some of it,
and vigorous self‑seeders put up volunteers in the bed next year. If that is a
problem, cut some heads and leave others rather than choosing between all and
nothing.

Seedheads also stand up to snow better than most people expect, and they are one
of the few things in the garden with any shape to them in January.
