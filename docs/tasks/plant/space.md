#### Spacing

Spacing is how far apart plants go at planting time, measured from one crown to
the next.

Use the plant’s mature spread as the gap. A perennial listed at 2ft wide goes 2ft
from its neighbor, center to center. That looks thin the first year and right by
the third, which is the trade every planting makes.

The reason to hold the spacing is airflow. Leaves that stay wet after rain or dew
are where powdery mildew and leaf spot begin, and plants set close enough to
touch stay wet for hours longer than plants with a gap between them. Some
species carry that risk more than others, and for those the spacing is not
optional.

Planted too close, they compete for water and light as well, and the weaker one
thins out and dies rather than both staying small. Planted too far apart, you
get bare soil and the weeds that follow, which mulch solves in the meantime.

Erring wide is the safer error. A gap closes on its own.
