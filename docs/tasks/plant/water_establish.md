#### Water to establish

A newly planted perennial has roots only where its pot was. Watering to
establish keeps that small root ball damp until the roots reach out into the
surrounding soil.

Water it in at planting, then keep the root zone damp, not wet, through the
first season. Check by pushing a finger into the soil next to the plant: damp at
2in down means wait. Water at the base rather than over the leaves, and water
slowly enough that it soaks in instead of running off.

Perennials are usually established by the end of their first full season, shrubs
by the end of their second. After that they move on to deep, infrequent
watering.

A plant that dries out during this period does not usually die at once. It sulks
and stays small, sometimes for years. Overwatering is the other failure, and it
looks the same from above: leaves yellow and drop, and the roots rot in ground
that never drains.

The finger test settles which one you are looking at, and takes a moment.

##### Tools
- Watering can or hose with a gentle rose
- Mulch, to slow evaporation
