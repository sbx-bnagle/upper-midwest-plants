#### Stake

Staking is putting support in place for a plant whose stems will not hold
themselves up.

Set the support in mid to late spring, when the plant is about a third of its
final height and you can still see the ground around it. Push stakes in outside
the crown so you are not driving them through the roots, and tie loosely, in a
figure of eight, so the stem has room to thicken. A ring or grid support is
easier than individual stakes for anything that comes up as a clump, and the
foliage hides it by the time it is needed.

Waiting until a plant has already gone over is the common mistake. Stems that
have bent do not straighten, and tying them upright afterwards leaves a bundle
rather than a plant. Tie too tightly and the tie cuts into the stem as it grows.

Some plants that flop can be pinched in early summer instead, which shortens
them and removes the need for support. Either approach works.

##### Tools
- Stakes, or a ring or grid support
- Soft twine or plant ties
- Mallet, for stakes in firm ground
