#### Contain spread

Some plants spread sideways, by underground runners or by suckers coming up from
the roots. Containing is cutting that spread back to the footprint you want.

Once a year, walk the edge of the planting and decide where it should stop. Then
push a spade straight down through the soil along that line, right around the
plant, to sever anything crossing it. Lift the severed runners out on the
outside of the line. For suckering shrubs, cut each sucker off below ground
where it joins the root, rather than at soil level, or it comes back doubled.

Spring is the usual time, before the plant has put on its growth for the year.
The runners are easier to find and there is less to pull out. A second pass in
midsummer is worth it on anything vigorous.

The alternative is a barrier sunk 12–18in at planting time, which works and
saves the annual pass, but only if it goes in before the plant does.

Skip this for a season on a genuinely running plant and it will be into the
neighboring clumps, where you then have to dig both up to separate them. Skip
it on a slow spreader and nothing much happens for years.

The plant is not damaged by any of this. You are removing growth it made in
addition to itself, not taking anything from the parent.

##### Tools
- Spade, sharp
- Garden fork
- Hand pruners for suckers
- Gloves
