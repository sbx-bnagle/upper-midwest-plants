#### Leave stems standing

Leaving stems standing means not cutting the plant back in fall, and letting
the dead stalks stand through the winter.

Stem‑nesting bees spend the winter inside hollow and pithy stems, and moth and
butterfly pupae overwinter attached to standing stalks. Cut the stems in
October and that generation goes out with the yard waste. Leave them and the
insects emerge in spring, at which point the stems have done their job and can
come down.

The stems also catch snow, which insulates the crown beneath them, and they hold
the shape of the planting through a season when nothing else does.

When you do cut, in spring, leave 8–24in of stubble rather than going flush to
the ground. Cut stems at that height are what the next generation of bees nests
in.

The cost of leaving them is that the bed looks unkempt from November to March.
That is the whole cost.
